Data and privacy

Ok, Name One — what the game stores, and why.

Last updated: 4 August 2026

Ok, Name One keeps a record of the games people play on it. This page says exactly what that record contains. It is written to be read rather than to be legally survivable, so it is short and it is specific.

There are no accounts, no logins, no email addresses and no passwords. You never tell the game who you are, and it never asks.

What identifies you

When you first open the game your browser generates a random ID and stores it locally. That ID is what the game uses to tell one player from another during a match, and it is what the stored games are keyed on.

It identifies a browser, not a person. Clearing your site data, using a different browser, using a different device, or simply leaving it a week on an iPhone — Safari evicts unused storage — all produce a new one. There is nothing behind it: no profile, no account, nothing that links it to you or to any other ID it replaced.

What is stored when you play

Nothing is stored while you are just sitting in a room. A record is written when a match actually starts, and it is added to as each round finishes. It contains:

  • The random browser ID above, and the name and avatar you typed for that game.
  • The room code, when the match started and ended, the settings it was played on, and whether it was finished or abandoned.
  • Which categories were on the ballot, which ones you voted for and how many votes you put on each, and which ones ended up being played.
  • Every word you typed, exactly as you typed it — including blanks and repeats that never scored — along with how many seconds into the round you sent it and whether anybody else wrote the same thing.
  • Your score and placing for each round, and your final placing for the match. If you played in a team, which team, and what it was called.

The names and the words are stored as typed. If you type something personal into the game — your real full name, an address, anything about somebody else — it is saved along with everything else. Treat what you type as something that gets kept.

What is not stored

  • Your IP address. It is seen briefly, in memory, to limit how many times one connection can hammer the server, and it is not written down or retained.
  • Your browser or device details, and what country you are in.
  • Anything at all about you outside of a game you played.

There are no advertising or analytics trackers on the game, and no third-party cookies. The only thing kept in your browser's storage is the random ID, your name and avatar so you do not have to retype them, and — if you are the device hosting a room — the code of that room, so that refreshing the page does not strand everybody.

What it is used for

Right now, nothing. The game never reads this record back. It is written after play has already happened, it does not affect any match, and if the database vanished tomorrow the game would play identically.

The intention is to use it for game statistics, for fun — the sort of thing that is only interesting in aggregate. Which categories the room picks when given the choice, which answers everybody reaches for and which ones nobody does, whether people play better in the first round or the last.

It is not used to identify anybody, to build a profile of anybody, or to work out who a player is. It is not sold, rented, shared or handed to anyone else, and there is no arrangement under which it would be.

How long it is kept

Indefinitely. Nothing deletes it automatically and there is no expiry — the record of a game played today is intended to still be there in a year, because a year of games is what makes the statistics worth having.

Getting your data removed

Honestly: there is no way to do this, and the reason is the same reason the record is fairly harmless. Nothing connects the random ID to you. There is no lookup, no account to log into and no field to search — so there is no way to find your rows in order to show you them or delete them, and no way to prove which ones are yours.

What you can do is stop the connection at your end: clearing the site's data in your browser discards the ID, and everything after that is recorded under a new one that nothing ties to the old.

Where it lives

The record is held in a Cloudflare D1 database. The game itself runs on Cloudflare and Vercel, who process the traffic in order to serve the game to you. Nobody else has access to any of it.

If this page and the game ever disagree, the page is the one that is wrong — tell whoever is running your room, and it will be fixed.
